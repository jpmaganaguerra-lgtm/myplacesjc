Things to Do in San José del Cabo: Art, Beaches and Wildlife

The best things to do in San José del Cabo mix art, water and open space: wander the historic center and its galleries, swim at a sheltered beach, watch birds at the estuary at dawn, play a round of golf, and, between December and April, watch for whales. It is a town that rewards a slower itinerary, and the main sights are close enough to combine in a single day.

Below is a guide to each, with the practical details that help you plan, and a three-day itinerary at the end. Details such as hours and seasons come from published local sources, so confirm them before you go, especially for events.

At a glance

  • Art Walk: Thursday evenings from November through June, 5 to 9 p.m., in the Gallery District.
  • Whale season: December through April, with the peak from January to March.
  • Calm-water swimming: Palmilla Beach, a sheltered, crescent-shaped bay.
  • Wildlife: the San José Estuary, best at dawn.
  • Nearby: Cabo San Lucas is about a half-hour drive, and Los Barriles on the East Cape about an hour.

Start in the historic center

Plaza Mijares, the main square, is the natural starting point. It is next to City Hall, the church and the Casa de la Cultura, and it opens onto the Gallery District, a walkable grid of cobblestone streets with art galleries, restaurants and boutique hotels.

Look for the Misión San José del Cabo, which gave the town its name. It was dedicated to Saint Joseph and first built in 1730, was destroyed in 1734 during an uprising by the Pericú people, and was rebuilt. The building you see today dates to 1918, and it remains an active parish, so be respectful if a service is underway.

Give the galleries an hour or two. Many of them show contemporary Mexican art, and the pace is relaxed enough to browse without feeling rushed. Afterward, find an open-air table on one of the nearby streets for lunch.

The Thursday Art Walk

The Art Walk is the town's best-known weekly event. It runs on Thursday evenings from November through June, from 5 to 9 p.m., and the galleries cluster along a two-block stretch of Calle Álvaro Obregón between Calle Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla and Calle Vicente Guerrero, starting about a block from Plaza Mijares. The event extends onto nearby side streets, so it is easy to wander from one gallery to the next and stop for a meal along the way.

Arrive around the start for the quietest browsing and plan dinner nearby. If your trip falls outside the season, the galleries still make a good daytime stop, though the evening atmosphere is part of the draw.

Beaches: where to swim and where to watch the surf

San José del Cabo has more swimmable beaches than Cabo San Lucas, and two names matter most.

Palmilla Beach is a sheltered, crescent-shaped bay with calm water, which makes it the easy choice for swimming and snorkeling. Rental umbrellas, palapa huts and kayak rentals are available, local fishermen work the shoreline, and beach restaurants serve fresh fish. Go early in the day for more space on the sand.

Costa Azul is a long beach with big, rolling waves, backed by hotels and restaurants. It is the area where surfers head, and it is better for watching the waves than for a casual swim. Check conditions and any posted warnings before you go in, and stay out of the water if you are not a strong swimmer.

The San José Estuary at dawn

Close to the Costa Azul hotel zone, the San José Estuary is a protected area where cars are not allowed. A paved path runs through part of it, and visitors can walk, hike or kayak. It is home to a wide range of birds, fish, turtles and other wildlife.

Dawn is the best time to go. The light is soft, the air is cool and the birds are most active. Bring water, a hat and binoculars if you have them, and keep your distance from nesting areas so the wildlife stays undisturbed.

Golf with a view

If you play, San José del Cabo is a good place to do it. Club Campestre San José is an 18-hole, par-71 Nicklaus Design course in desert foothills overlooking the Sea of Cortez, about 15 minutes from the airport. Visitors are welcome, and tee times must be booked in advance. We cover tees, booking and heat in our guide to golf in San José del Cabo.

Whale season

From December through April, whales migrate to the sheltered waters off Baja California Sur. Humpback and gray whales are the ones most visitors see, and blue, fin and orca sightings are possible but less common. January through March is the peak, and February offers the best odds of close encounters.

Many tours depart from the marina in Cabo San Lucas, about a half-hour drive from San José del Cabo. Early-morning departures often have calmer seas and softer light. Choose an operator that follows responsible-viewing practices, keep a respectful distance, and bring a light jacket and sunscreen, since breezes on the water can be cool.

Day trips beyond town

  • Cabo San Lucas: about a half-hour drive for the marina, boat tours and dinner.
  • Los Barriles and the East Cape: roughly an hour from San José del Cabo, for a change of scenery on the Sea of Cortez side.
  • Todos Santos and the Sierra de la Laguna: excursions that trade the coast for a Pacific-side town and mountain scenery.
  • Desert dunes: sandboarding is a popular excursion for travelers who want something more active.

Book day trips through licensed operators, and ask about pickup points, what is included and how long the transfer takes, so you can fit them around your other plans.

A three-day plan

  1. Day 1, the center: a morning walk around Plaza Mijares and the mission, galleries and lunch, then, on a Thursday in season, the Art Walk and dinner nearby.
  2. Day 2, golf and the beach: an early tee time, lunch, then an afternoon swim at Palmilla. If you do not play, spend the morning at the pool or on the beach.
  3. Day 3, wildlife and a day trip: the estuary at dawn, then in season a whale-watching tour from Cabo San Lucas, or an excursion to Todos Santos or the dunes.
