San José del Cabo has an 18-hole Nicklaus Design course, Club Campestre San José, set in rolling desert foothills above the Sea of Cortez and about 15 minutes from Los Cabos International Airport. Visitors are welcome on weekdays and weekends, tee times must be booked in advance, and green fees are not published, so you contact the club for current rates.

This guide covers what to know before you tee off: the course layout, which tees to choose, how to book, how to handle the heat and how to build the rest of your day around a round. The course details come from published golf listings and the local tourism board, so confirm rates, hours and policies directly with the club before you go.

## The course in brief

The fairways run through rolling foothills. Bunkers are strategically placed, and arroyos (dry creek beds) wind across the fairways, so course management counts as much as distance. The greens are the signature. They are undulating and often multi-tiered, and local tourism listings describe them as some of the most contoured in Los Cabos.

That has a practical consequence. On a course like this, the shot into the green matters less than where you leave your approach. Leaving the ball below the hole is usually worth more than adding distance, and a quiet putting-green warm-up before your round pays off, because the speed and slope of the greens reward players who have already felt them.

## Choosing your tees

The course plays 6,966 yards from the back tees, 6,093 from the club tees and 4,917 from the front tees, all at par 71. The spread is wide enough that a mixed group can play together without anyone being overmatched.

-   **Back tees (6,966 yards):** for confident, low-handicap players who want the full test.
-   **Club tees (6,093 yards):** a sensible default for most visiting golfers, and the right choice if you want to enjoy the views and keep the pace moving.
-   **Front tees (4,917 yards):** a good fit for newer players, seniors and anyone coming back from time off, and for a family round with mixed experience.

If you are unsure, move up one set of tees. A course with bunkers and arroyos is more fun when you reach the greens in regulation than when you spend the day recovering.

## Booking a tee time

The course asks guests to book in advance, and it does not publish green fees online. Plan to contact the pro shop directly, and ask for the details that change the real price of a round. A short list helps:

-   What the green fee includes, such as a cart, range balls or a yardage book.
-   Whether there are twilight or afternoon rates and when they start.
-   Whether club rentals are available and how far ahead to reserve them.
-   The cancellation policy, in case a hot day or a hurricane-season forecast changes your plans.
-   The best tee times for your group size, and whether groups of two or four are easier to place.

High season in Los Cabos runs from November through June. Those months are the busiest for tee times, so reserve earlier if your dates include holidays or weekends.

## Beating the heat

Baja California Sur is desert, and the sun is strong most of the year. In summer the heat is serious, and in the shoulder seasons it can still surprise you by midday. A few habits make a round more enjoyable and safer:

-   Book the earliest available tee time. Mornings are cooler, the course is quieter and you finish in time for lunch.
-   Carry more water than you think you need, and keep drinking it from the first hole rather than waiting until you are thirsty.
-   Wear a hat, sun-protective clothing and sunscreen that you reapply at the turn.
-   If you are not used to the climate, ride in a cart and keep your first round shorter or on the front tees.

## Building the rest of the day around a round

A morning round leaves plenty of day, and San José del Cabo makes it easy to fill. Many golfers head to a nearby beach after the round. Palmilla, a sheltered, crescent-shaped bay with calm water, is the usual choice for a swim or a snorkel, with beach restaurants serving fresh fish nearby.

On Thursday evenings from November through June, the galleries downtown open for the Art Walk, which runs from 5 to 9 p.m. around Calle Álvaro Obregón, about a block from Plaza Mijares. Pair a morning round with an evening stroll through the galleries, and you have a full day without a long drive.

If you travel with non-golfers, the same logic applies in reverse. They can spend the morning at the pool or the beach, join you for lunch and meet up for dinner downtown. San José del Cabo works well for mixed groups because everything is within a short drive.

## Other golf in Los Cabos

Los Cabos is one of Mexico's best-known golf destinations, with additional courses along the Tourist Corridor between San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas. That corridor is roughly 20 to 40 minutes from the airport, so if you want to play more than one course in a week, factor in transfer times and book each tee time before you finalize your lodging. Staying in San José del Cabo keeps a round at Club Campestre San José to a short drive, and leaves the other courses within reach.
