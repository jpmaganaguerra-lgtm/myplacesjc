For most travelers, the best place to stay in San José del Cabo is the one that matches how they plan to spend their days. Stay downtown if you want to walk to galleries and restaurants, near the hotel zone and the estuary if the beach comes first, and near the golf course if you want more space, a kitchen and an easy morning tee time. San José del Cabo is calmer and more local than its neighbor, Cabo San Lucas, and that difference shapes every choice below.

This guide explains how the main areas compare, what to weigh between a hotel room and a suite with a kitchen, and how to time your trip. Distances and seasons come from published local sources and are approximate, so confirm details such as hours and schedules before you travel.

## San José del Cabo or Cabo San Lucas?

The two towns sit about a half-hour apart and feel very different. Cabo San Lucas is built around the marina and a busy nightlife strip. San José del Cabo is older, with a colonial-style plaza, a mission church and a walkable gallery district. It also has more beaches that are suitable for swimming; many of the beaches around Cabo San Lucas are known for strong currents and surf that make swimming unsafe.

That does not mean you have to choose one or the other. From San José del Cabo you can reach Cabo San Lucas for a day trip, a boat tour or dinner, then return to a quieter base. Travelers who want fewer crowds, longer breakfasts and a destination that feels lived in tend to prefer staying in San José del Cabo.

## The historic center: walk everywhere

Downtown revolves around Plaza Mijares, the main square, which is close to City Hall, the church and the Casa de la Cultura. It is also the gateway to the Gallery District, a compact area of cobblestone streets where art galleries, restaurants and boutique hotels sit within walking distance of one another.

Staying here means you can leave the car parked. Dinner, a gallery stop and a walk back to your room fit into one evening. On Thursday evenings from November through June, the galleries along Calle Álvaro Obregón open for the weekly Art Walk, which runs from 5 to 9 p.m. Downtown is also where you feel San José del Cabo's history most clearly: the town takes its name from the mission, first built in 1730 and rebuilt in its current form in 1918.

The trade-off is the beach. Downtown is not on the sand, so plan on a short drive or taxi ride for swimming, and expect more evening activity around the plaza on high-season weekends.

## Near the hotel zone and the estuary

On the coast, a short drive from downtown, the Costa Azul hotel zone is where you find beachfront hotels and restaurants backed by a long stretch of sand. The San José Estuary is right there too. It is a protected area with no car traffic, where visitors can walk, kayak and watch birds, and dawn is the best time for wildlife.

This area suits travelers who want the beach in front of them and are happy to take a short ride into town for dinner. Keep in mind that the waves at Costa Azul can be large, so it is better for watching surfers than for casual swimming. For a calmer swim, the sheltered bay at Palmilla is a short drive away.

## Near the golf course: space, quiet and a kitchen

A third option is a base near the golf course, close to both the historic center and the beaches. It is a practical choice for travelers who want to do a little of everything without living out of a suitcase in a single hotel room.

Staying near the course works well for golfers who want to be on the first tee early, and for families or groups who prefer a living room and a full kitchen to a standard hotel room. You trade a little walkability for room to spread out. Beach and downtown outings are a short drive away, by taxi, transfer or rental car.

## Hotel room or suite with a kitchen?

This choice matters more than the neighborhood for many trips. A hotel room is simple and efficient for a short stay. A suite or residence with a kitchen starts to make sense as the group, or the length of the stay, grows.

-   **Stays of five nights or more:** a kitchen lets you do a grocery run, cook breakfast and keep drinks cold, which adds up over a week.
-   **Families and groups:** a living room and separate bedrooms give everyone space, and connecting units can combine into larger layouts.
-   **Two travelers on a long weekend:** a hotel room is usually enough, particularly if you plan to eat out.
-   **Remote workers:** a living area and a terrace make a bigger difference than another few square feet of bedroom.

When you compare properties, check the sleeping arrangements (king or two queens), whether the kitchen is full or just a kitchenette, and what is included on the property, such as pools, parking and a front desk that is staffed around the clock.

## When to go

High season runs from November through June. That is when the Thursday Art Walk operates and, from December to April, when whales migrate through the region, with the best chances generally from January to March. Book earlier for holiday weeks and for the peak whale months.

Summer is hot, and the Pacific hurricane season runs from mid-May to November, with the highest risk around late summer and early fall. Travelers who visit then often find lower rates and fewer crowds, but should watch forecasts and buy flexible or refundable bookings.

## Getting around

San José del Cabo is easy to reach: expect roughly 15 to 25 minutes from the airport, compared with about 35 to 50 minutes to Cabo San Lucas and 20 to 40 minutes to the Tourist Corridor, which links the two towns. Traffic and time of day can change those numbers.

If you plan to stay mostly in one area, transfers and taxis may be enough. If you want to combine downtown, beaches, golf and a day trip, renting a car for part of the stay gives you the most freedom. Ask any property you are considering whether parking is on site.
